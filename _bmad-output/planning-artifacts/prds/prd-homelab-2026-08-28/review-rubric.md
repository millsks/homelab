# PRD Quality Review — Project Asgard (prd-homelab-2026-08-28)

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely good PRD: it has an explicit thesis (the Runbook/Automation convergence invariant in §1 and §3 *Converged*), a Non-Goals section (§6) that does real work, counter-metrics (SM-C1–C4) that name things the operator must *not* optimize, and a Glossary (§3) disciplined enough that downstream artifacts can source-extract from it. It is calibrated correctly for a single-operator platform — the shape is a capability spec, not a consumer PRD, and it says so.

What is at risk is the testability of exactly the requirement the document is built around. FR-1 quantifies over "any operational activity in Asgard" with no enumeration of that set anywhere, which means SM-1 and SM-2 — both primary metrics — have no denominator. Alongside that sit two internal contradictions the architecture workflow will hit immediately (NFR-11's blanket TLS claim against the PRD's own NFS design; FR-34's bypass-prevention against §6 "Not segmented"), and a systematic pattern of deferring every numeric threshold to "a defined period" without naming who defines it.

Fix the FR-1 denominator, reconcile the two contradictions, and set the deferred thresholds — that is a short punch list, not a rewrite. The document is close to build-ready.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and the trade-offs name what was given up. §6 gives every Non-Goal its consequence rather than just its absence — "Not segmented" explains that the lab shares the household LAN, "Not remotely accessible" explains that this "removes the plane in which an identity-dependent remote path could lock the operator out." §10 Risks says "Accepted deliberately" three times rather than inventing a mitigation, and the out-of-band-management row concludes "Residual risk accepted." That is the opposite of the smoothed-to-neutral failure mode.

The best passages are the FR Notes. FR-23's note explicitly refuses a framing the reader might bring: "This is not a defense layer weighed against FR-24 — it is a single configuration setting with no ongoing cost." FR-65's note argues *against* the intuitive answer: "Leaving wireless enabled as a fallback is worse than not having it, because silent failover destabilises cluster membership while every node still appears up." FR-25's note distinguishes the real justification from the plausible one: "The justification is service availability, not administrative access." A reader pushing back on any of these finds their objection already answered.

Where it slips is callout placement. Both `[NOTE FOR PM]` markers sit in §7.2 at items already decided and already reversible — PostgreSQL HA and remote access. Neither of the document's actual live tensions carries one.

### Findings

- **medium** PM callouts sit at safe checkpoints (§7.2, lines 872 and 875) — The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` markers are attached to a deferred nice-to-have and to a decision the PRD itself calls reversible "without disturbing anything else." Meanwhile NFR-11 makes a claim the design cannot satisfy, FR-34 requires something §6 forecloses, and OQ-6 admits FR-51 may have no satisfiable answer — none flagged. *Fix:* move or add `[NOTE FOR PM]` to NFR-11, FR-34, and FR-51/OQ-6; drop the one on remote access, which §6, §7.2, and §10 already settle three times over.
- **medium** Two Open Questions are epic gates but §9 does not treat them as such (§11 items 5 and 6, §9 Phase 1) — OQ-5 gates a hardware purchase ("must appear on Synology's supported list before purchase — DSM is the NUT Server, so incompatibility forces a topology change rather than a workaround"), which blocks epic D8 entirely. OQ-6 asks which channel delivers alerts, which FR-51 requires and which D6, D8, and D9 all depend on. The delivery plan lists both epics with no gate marker. *Fix:* add a "Blocked by" column to the Phase 1 table naming OQ-5 against D8 and OQ-6 against D6.
- **low** OQ-1 answers itself (§11 item 1) — "FreeIPA is the leading candidate because it delivers FR-21, FR-25, and the whole of §4.6 as one system rather than three. Resolved in architecture." The criterion is useful; the question is not open. *Fix:* restate as a decision with a named condition for revisiting, or move to §12 as an assumption.

## Substance over theater — adequate

Almost nothing here is furniture. The Vision (§1) could not be dropped into another homelab PRD — "small enough to sit on a shelf and honest enough to teach," and the governing invariant that "following the runbook by hand must produce a node the automation then considers already converged, which makes drift between the two a detectable defect rather than a slow decay" is a specific, falsifiable design commitment, not a mood. There is exactly one persona, correctly. The differentiation claim ("it is described, not just built") is earned by being promoted to §4.1, the *first* feature rather than a documentation appendix. FR-24's third bullet — that a local Break-glass home nested under an NFS mount point "is shadowed by the mount while it is active and unreachable while it hangs" — is hard-won operational detail no template produces.

The exception is §5. The NFR section is where the boilerplate reflex shows, and one of its entries is worse than boilerplate — it is contradicted by the PRD's own design.

### Findings

- **high** NFR-11 claims universal internal TLS that the PRD's own storage and cache design do not deliver (§5.3) — "All service-to-service and operator-to-service traffic within Asgard is encrypted with certificates chaining to Draupnir." But FR-15 and FR-17 put Home Directories and Persistent Volumes on NFS Shares, which carry no TLS; FR-48 requires only that "Redis requires authentication," not that it is encrypted. As written, architecture must either violate a stated NFR or invent transport security the PRD never scoped. This is the classic "everything is encrypted" NFR that silently fails. *Fix:* rewrite as a bounded claim — all HTTP/S and administrative interfaces, plus LDAP, present certificates chaining to Draupnir — and add an explicit carve-out naming NFS and Redis as cleartext-on-trusted-LAN, with the §6 "Not segmented" decision as the justification.
- **medium** NFR-1 is the differentiating quality attribute and has no test (§5.1) — "Every Procedure is executable by the operator six months later with no recall of building it. Ambiguity is a defect." The condition cannot be evaluated until six months have passed, and the PRD offers no proxy. This matters because the whole document is staked on documentation quality; every other differentiating property (FR-2 convergence, FR-60 Drill, FR-63 verified restore) got a concrete test and this one did not. *Fix:* give it a proxy test — e.g. a Procedure is accepted only after a cold execution in which the operator follows the Runbook without consulting the Automation or prior notes, matching the standard SM-1 already applies to one Node.
- **low** NFR-17 is an unfalsifiable universal negative (§5.4) — "No unattended failure results in data corruption." It reads as an aspiration but is actually doing useful work as a priority ordering, which the second sentence states well ("Availability may be sacrificed; integrity may not"). *Fix:* lead with the ordering and demote the absolute to a design principle, so it is not mistaken for a testable NFR.
- **low** UJ-6 drives no requirement that FR-9 and FR-45 did not already carry (§2.3) — Snapshot, break, roll back. It is the thinnest of the six, and the "Resolution" line ("the snapshot discipline is what makes the lab a place to learn rather than a place to be careful") restates the Vision rather than adding a requirement. Compare UJ-4, which is the sole origin of the FR-57 ordering constraint. *Fix:* keep it — it costs little — but recognize the Climax/Resolution scaffolding is load-bearing in UJ-1/2/4 and decorative in UJ-3/UJ-6.

## Strategic coherence — strong

There is a thesis and the document is arranged around it. Procedure Discipline (§4.1) is deliberately placed first, and the PRD says why: "the property that distinguishes Asgard from an ordinary homelab, and therefore the first feature rather than an afterthought." Everything downstream of that follows — FR-4 makes the Repository authoritative, FR-42 makes reconciliation the enforcement mechanism ("which makes the Repository's status as source of truth (FR-4) enforced rather than declared"), and §9's two named gates test the two halves of the thesis separately: "D11 validates the documentation; S7 validates the architecture."

The metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity. SM-1 measures undocumented steps, SM-2 measures convergence — both are direct tests of the central claim. The counter-metrics are the strongest evidence of coherence in the document: SM-C2 declares uptime an anti-signal ("A long uninterrupted uptime is a sign of insufficient experimentation"), SM-C4 forbids optimizing rebuild time, and SM-C1 names the specific way SM-1 and SM-2 could be gamed. Very few PRDs name the failure mode of their own metrics.

The MVP scope kind is platform, and the Walking Skeleton logic matches it correctly — thin across every seam first, because "integration seams are where this class of build fails." The weakness is on the far side of that gate.

### Findings

- **medium** Phase 1 has no ordering, no must/should split, and no cut line (§9, D1–D10) — Phase 0 is rigorously sequenced with a stated gate; Phase 1 is ten epics in a table with no dependencies, no priority, and nothing said about what happens if the operator stalls at D5 on a Tuesday in month three. For a solo evenings-and-weekends build this is the most likely real failure mode, and it is the one part of the delivery plan that reads as a backlog with a heading. FR-25's note is the *only* place in the document where a v2 cut line is named. *Fix:* mark D11 and D8/D9 as must-have (they carry SM-1, SM-5, SM-6), mark the rest as ordered-but-cuttable, and state which epics can slip to v2 without invalidating the Phase 0 gate.
- **low** §7.1 describes v1-complete, not a minimum (§7) — Fifteen bullets covering every feature in §4. Nothing in §7.1 could be removed and still be called the same product, which means the section is a restatement of §4 rather than a scope decision. §7.2 defers only things that were never wanted. *Fix:* either rename §7 to "v1 Scope" and stop implying a minimum, or identify the two or three §7.1 lines that are genuinely the floor.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The good FRs here are very good. FR-2 is the model: "Running Automation against a Runbook-built system produces zero changes" is binary, mechanical, and cannot be argued about. FR-23 includes a negative test most PRDs omit — "A never-before-seen Account correctly fails." FR-35 is the best-quantified requirement in the document, giving both a bound and a verification against configuration drift: "The 15-minute bound is stated in each Relying Party's configuration, not assumed." FR-57's ordering is fully specified and FR-60 forecloses the cheap answer: "Measured runtime under real load is recorded, not taken from the specification sheet." FR-63's "Restored data is verified correct, not merely present" and NFR-18's "Configured-but-untested is treated as not present" do the same job at the NFR layer.

Against that, three patterns undercut the rest. First, and most seriously, FR-1 asserts a property over a set the PRD never enumerates. Second, every numeric threshold in the document except the 15-minute token bound and the 20 GB RAM floor has been deferred to a phrase — "a defined threshold," "a defined period," "a bounded interval," "a defined schedule," "with margin" — with no statement of where those numbers get set or who owns them. §0 says mechanism belongs to architecture, but a retention window and a backup schedule are requirements, not mechanisms. Third, two FRs offer disjunctive consequences where both branches pass, which makes them unfalsifiable.

### Findings

- **critical** FR-1 quantifies over an unenumerated set, and both primary metrics inherit the gap (§4.1, FR-1; SM-1; SM-2) — "for any operational activity in Asgard, find both a Runbook and its corresponding Automation," with the consequence "No operational activity is documented in only one form." Nothing in the PRD defines or requires an enumeration of operational activities, so there is no denominator: an engineer cannot compute coverage, a reviewer cannot fail the requirement, and SM-2's "for every Procedure" and SM-1's "zero undocumented steps" both rest on a set that does not exist as an artifact. This is the document's thesis requirement and it is the least testable one in it. *Fix:* add an FR requiring a Procedure Index in the Repository that enumerates every operational activity, with FR-1 restated as "every entry in the Procedure Index has both forms" and SM-2 as "for every entry in the Procedure Index." Adding an activity to the platform without adding an Index entry then becomes the detectable defect.
- **high** FR-34's bypass consequence is foreclosed by §6 "Not segmented" (§4.7, FR-34; §6) — "The service is not reachable bypassing the proxy." The PRD rules out VLANs, a dedicated firewall, and network zoning, and places everything on a flat household LAN alongside non-Asgard devices. On that topology any host on the LAN can reach the backing service's port directly, and no FR or NFR anywhere requires host-level firewalling on Nodes or Guests. As written the requirement is unsatisfiable within the stated scope, and architecture will have to either introduce unscoped network controls or quietly drop the consequence. *Fix:* add a host-level firewall requirement (backing services bind only to the proxy's source, or a per-host deny rule) as an explicit FR, and note in §6 that "Not segmented" refers to network zoning rather than to host firewalling.
- **medium** Numeric thresholds are systematically deferred with no owner named (FR-19, FR-29, FR-42, FR-47, FR-49, FR-50, FR-56, FR-61) — "an alert fires at a defined threshold," "Retention is a defined period," "converge within a bounded interval," "Backups run on a defined schedule," "Shutdown begins automatically after a defined time on battery." Eight requirements, no numbers, and no statement anywhere that architecture is responsible for setting them. Contrast FR-35 (15 minutes) and NFR-6 (20 GB), which show the PRD is capable of committing. Downstream story creation will have to invent these or block. *Fix:* either set the values here, or add one line to §0 stating that thresholds phrased as "defined" are set in the architecture spine and recorded in the Repository, and list them so none is missed.
- **medium** FR-56 dropped a quantified budget that already existed upstream (§4.13, FR-56) — "The Shutdown Sequence completes within the battery's proven runtime, with margin." The addendum §9 carries a concrete design: a ~5 minute target against 8–12 minutes of measured runtime, with a per-phase budget table. The PRD replaced a number with an adjective, and SM-6 ("a completed Shutdown Sequence, Nidavellir last, no corruption") inherits no time bound at all. This is the one place the PRD is *less* specific than its own source. *Fix:* restate as "the Shutdown Sequence completes within 5 minutes, against a measured runtime of not less than 8 minutes at real load," and let §12's assumption on FR-56 continue to cover the measurement.
- **medium** FR-4 and FR-42 offer disjunctive consequences where both branches pass (§4.1, FR-4; §4.9, FR-42) — "A change made directly on a running system is either reflected back into the Repository or reverted by the next Automation run" and "Drift introduced directly against Yggdrasil is reverted or reported." Reverted and reported are opposite behaviours with opposite operational consequences; a system doing either satisfies the FR, so neither can be failed. FR-4 in particular is the source-of-truth requirement, where the answer matters most. *Fix:* pick one per FR. For FR-4 the thesis argues for reverted; for FR-42 state the condition under which each applies (e.g. reverted for declared resources, reported for undeclared ones).
- **medium** "session window" is used as if defined and is not (§2.3 UJ-5; §4.7, FR-32) — FR-32's "One authentication covers subsequent interfaces within the session window" and UJ-5's "stops honoring existing sessions within the configured window." FR-35 defines a 15-minute *access token* lifetime, which is not the same thing as an SSO session lifetime — a Relying Party fronted by the FR-34 proxy can hold a session cookie that outlives the token, and FR-35's third consequence ("A session active at the moment of disablement loses access within the window") depends on the two being aligned. Neither the Glossary nor §5 defines it. *Fix:* add **Session Window** to §3 with a value, and add a consequence to FR-34 requiring the proxy's session lifetime to not exceed it.
- **medium** No recovery point objective or backup retention depth anywhere (§4.14, FR-61; §5.4) — FR-61 requires backups "on a defined schedule" and FR-63 requires verified restores, but the PRD never says how much data loss is acceptable or how many restore points are kept. For a platform whose stated deliverable is restore evidence, "how far back can I go, and how much do I lose" are the two questions a restore Drill actually answers. NFR-17 forbids corruption but is silent on loss. *Fix:* add an NFR to §5.4 stating an RPO per data class (PostgreSQL, Persistent Volumes, Directory, Guests) and a retention depth, and reference it from FR-61.
- **medium** FR-13 is satisfiable with both resolvers on one Node (§4.3, FR-13) — "More than one resolver answers for the domain. Loss of one resolver does not prevent Nodes or Guests from resolving names." Nothing requires the resolvers to occupy different failure domains, so two Guests on `odin` would pass while a Node loss takes out both. FR-38 gets this right for the Control Plane Guests ("No two Control Plane Guests share a Node") and FR-25 gets it right for the Directory; FR-13 was left behind. *Fix:* add "No two resolvers share a Node," mirroring FR-38.
- **low** Three consequences turn on undefined terms (FR-12, FR-30, FR-46) — FR-12's "Infrastructure components receive stable addresses" never says which components are infrastructure and which are not, leaving Guest addressing ambiguous; FR-30's "takes effect for IdP authentication immediately" uses an adjective where the rest of §4.7 uses bounds; FR-46's "Each Workload's database is isolated from others'" does not say whether isolation means a separate database, a separate role, or a separate instance. *Fix:* define the infrastructure set in FR-12 (or say "every Node, Guest, and published service" as FR-11 does), give FR-30 a bound, and name the isolation level in FR-46.

## Scope honesty — strong

§6 is the best section in the document. Nine Non-Goals, each stating not just the exclusion but what reversing it would cost: "Adding one changes the availability and UX requirements of the whole system," "This is what permits an internal certificate authority and a modest security posture," "Group-based authorization simulates separation; it does not enforce isolation between real parties." That last one is a genuinely honest admission that the multi-admin simulation in §2.2 is a simulation. §7.2 pairs every deferral with a reason and distinguishes deferred from rejected. §10 accepts three risks outright rather than manufacturing mitigations.

Open-items density is appropriate to the stakes: six Open Questions, six assumptions, two PM notes against 65 FRs. That is a document ready to be built from, not one still deciding.

Two things are less honest than the rest. FR-25's note leaves the Directory replica in an ambiguous state against §7.1, and the assumption convention promised in §0 was not actually applied.

### Findings

- **medium** FR-25 hedges toward v2 while §7.1 lists it as in scope (§4.5, FR-25; §7.1; §9 D2) — The note says it is "sequenced into the deepen phase rather than the Walking Skeleton, so it remains a clean single cut if deferred to v2," but §7.1 states "Directory with replica" as MVP scope with no conditional, and D2 lists it as a Phase 1 deliverable. The brief's addendum calls the replica a "should-have." So the PRD promoted a should-have to in-scope and then hedged it in a footnote, leaving the epic writer to guess. *Fix:* decide. If it is in v1, delete the "deferred to v2" clause; if it is conditional, mark it in §7.1 as such and state the condition.
- **medium** The `[ASSUMPTION]` convention promised in §0 is never applied inline (§0; §12) — "Inferences carry inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, indexed in §12." Zero inline tags exist in the body; §12 has six entries pointing back at sections. The index therefore only roundtrips one way, and a reader working through FR-17 or FR-32 has no signal at the point of use that the requirement rests on an unverified inference — which matters most for FR-32, whose assumption ("The hypervisor UI, Nidavellir, and the observability stack each support OIDC authentication against a self-hosted provider") could invalidate the FR outright. *Fix:* add the six inline tags at their cited locations, or amend §0 to describe §12 as a standalone index.

## Downstream usability — adequate

§0 names the downstream consumers explicitly — "the architecture workflow that will turn these requirements into a technical spine, the epic-and-story workflow that will decompose them" — so this is a chain-top PRD and traceability carries real weight.

The Glossary earns its keep. Terms are defined with their cardinality ("One Asgard," "Exactly four," "One Cluster"), with the distinctions that matter spelled out (Cluster vs Yggdrasil, "Distinct from Cluster; never used interchangeably"; Account lives in the Directory and is "Referenced by the IdP, never duplicated into it"; "Procedure — a Runbook and its Automation together. Neither alone is a Procedure"). Usage in §4 is consistent with it. FR IDs are unique with no gaps across 1–65, UJ IDs are contiguous, and most FRs carry explicit realizes-links back to UJs.

What is missing is the layer story creation actually needs: the epic tables in §9 name deliverables in prose but carry no FR IDs, so whoever decomposes D1–D11 must re-derive the FR-to-epic mapping by reading §4 and guessing. Combined with one wrong cross-reference in the metrics, this is the dimension with the most mechanical drag.

### Findings

- **medium** §9 epic tables carry no FR traceability (§9, Phase 0 and Phase 1) — "D2 | Identity depth | Directory replica, credential caching, Break-glass with local homes on every host, Group-driven authorization" clearly maps to FR-21, FR-23, FR-24, FR-25, but the mapping is never written down, and some are genuinely ambiguous: FR-26 (Accounts in the Repository) could sit in S4 or D2; FR-10 (Cluster manageable during Directory outage) has no obvious home in either phase. The epic workflow will reconstruct this and may not reconstruct it the same way. *Fix:* add an "FRs" column to both §9 tables covering all 65, which also surfaces any FR that no epic delivers.
- **medium** SM-10 cites FR-22 where NFR-22 is meant (§8, SM-10) — "The second application is boring. Deploying a second SSO-protected Workload requires no new Procedure. Validates FR-22, FR-44." FR-22 is "Disabling an Account revokes host access," which has nothing to do with repeating a deployment Procedure. NFR-22 — "Adding a Node, Guest, or Workload follows an existing Procedure rather than requiring a new one" — is the requirement SM-10 actually validates. *Fix:* change to "Validates NFR-22, FR-44."
- **low** Three small traceability frictions (§4.3 FR-65; §7.1 and §8 SM-4; §4.3 FR-11) — FR-65 sits between FR-13 and FR-14 rather than at the end, marking it as a late addition and breaking scan order; §7.1 and SM-4 use the bare name "Forseti" where §3 established **Identity Provider** as the term and `forseti` only as its host, which is exactly the synonym introduction §3 calls "a discipline violation"; FR-11's consequence "Names follow the Norse registry in the brief addendum" makes a testable condition depend on a document outside the requirements contract. *Fix:* move FR-65 after FR-64 or renumber; use "the IdP (`forseti`)" consistently; either inline the registry's rules into §3 or cite the addendum path explicitly as §0 does.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape is right and the PRD is self-aware about why. §2.3 states the calibration outright: "Operator journeys, downscaled per the single-role audience. They exist to keep requirements anchored to lived moments rather than to organize the document." Six UJs for a single-operator internal platform is at the edge of justified, but they earn it — UJ-4 is the sole origin of FR-57's ordering constraint, UJ-5 is the sole origin of FR-35's 15-minute bound, and UJ-1 anchors the entire §4.1 thesis. The document is organized as a capability spec with UJs as anchors, which is the correct inversion for this product.

Success Metrics are operational rather than user-facing (Drill outcomes, convergence results, restore evidence) — correct for a platform with one user and no adoption question. The absence of market analysis, personas, and monetization is the right call and is not a gap. §2.2 Non-Users does real work by ruling out the household and the public internet, which is what makes the modest security posture in §6 defensible rather than lazy.

No over-formalization is present. This is neither a consumer PRD wearing infrastructure clothes nor an infrastructure spec pretending to have users.

### Findings

- **low** UJ-4 has no named protagonist (§2.3) — "The power fails at three in the morning. Nobody is awake." Kevin appears only at the climax as a recipient. The other five open with him as the actor. Since UJ-4 is load-bearing for FR-56 through FR-60, the inconsistency is worth closing. *Fix:* reframe as "Kevin sleeps through a power failure" so the protagonist convention holds across all six.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity:** FR-1 through FR-65 are all present, unique, with no gaps or duplicates. UJ-1–6, SM-1–10, SM-C1–C4, and NFR-1–23 are likewise contiguous. Only FR-65 is out of sequence (placed between FR-13 and FR-14 in §4.3).
- **Cross-references:** one broken — SM-10's "FR-22" should be NFR-22. All other FR-to-FR references resolve (FR-2 from FR-7, FR-4 from FR-45, FR-18 from §4.10, FR-27 from FR-41, FR-32 from FR-52, FR-51 from FR-59/FR-61, FR-36/FR-50 from NFR-15). Every UJ is realized by at least one FR; every FR realizes-link points at a UJ that exists.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** broken in one direction. §12 has six entries; zero inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags exist in the body despite §0 promising them. Every §12 entry does point at a real section and FR.
- **Glossary drift:** low overall. "Forseti" used bare in §7.1 and SM-4 where §3 established **Identity Provider**; "Proxmox" appears in §7.1 despite §0's rule that technology choices live in the addendum (PostgreSQL and Redis appear throughout §4.10 for the same reason — defensible since the brief settled them, but inconsistent with the stated rule and worth one clarifying sentence in §0). "session window" and "Infrastructure components" are used as terms of art without definitions.
- **External references:** `infrastructure_inventory.txt` (§0) and the brief/addendum paths all exist and resolve. FR-11's reference to "the Norse registry in the brief addendum" resolves to addendum §3 but is not given a path.
- **Required sections:** all present and appropriate for the agreed stakes — Vision, Target User with JTBD and Non-Users, Glossary, Features with FRs, cross-cutting NFRs, Non-Goals, MVP Scope, Success Metrics with counter-metrics, Delivery Plan, Risks, Open Questions, Assumptions Index. Nothing expected for a single-operator platform PRD is missing.
